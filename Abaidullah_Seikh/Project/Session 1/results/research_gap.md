# Research gap for sparse-history financial recommendation

**Student:** Abaidullah Seikh  
**Course:** LLM4Rec, HSE University  
**Scope:** Task2 research preparation; pending comparison requirements, not an approved study design.

**Research question:** Do LLM-generated transaction-history summaries improve nDCG@10 for predicting future asset purchases compared with structured-history features for FAR-Trans users with sparse histories?

## Bounded gap supported by the inspected literature

> **Among the studies inspected, we did not find a same-information comparison of LLM-generated transaction-history summaries against structured-history features for a FAR-Trans cohort defined as sparse using only pre-cutoff history, evaluated on future-purchase nDCG@10 with matched users, candidate assets, item information and downstream ranking conditions.** Language-profile methods and same-dataset LLM systems supply relevant precedents, but do not establish this particular effect.

This statement is bounded to **FAR-Trans, FLARKO, ACL UPR, LangPTune, Investors Are (Not) Always Right, and Conv-FinRe**. It is not a claim that no such work exists anywhere or that the proposed experiment is the first. The core five are the first five; Conv-FinRe was investigated and retained as adjacent evaluation context. [literature_review.md](literature_review.md) explains the selection and findings; [reference_verification.md](reference_verification.md) records full bibliographies, access and version boundaries. ([FAR-Trans §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08692v1#S5); [FLARKO §§3–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15993v1#S3); [UPR §§3–5](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.753.pdf); [LangPTune v2 §4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2#S4.SS5); [Investors §§6–8](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3780097#sec-6); [Conv-FinRe §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16990v2#S3))

## Nearest method and nearest application answer different questions

### LangPTune is nearest in method

LangPTune generates a profile from short interaction histories, embeds it with Mxbai and ranks future items. Crucially, its **NoProfile** variants bypass generation and embed historical item metadata directly. The frozen-pretrained-decoder variants provide partial control over the ranking component. This is closer to the RQ than simply comparing an LLM system with collaborative filtering. ([v2 §§3.1, 4.2.1, 4.5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2#S4.SS5.SSS1))

**What it answers:** task-specific optimization can improve language-profile pipelines; direct-history and compressed-profile trade-offs can be measured; summaries need not win. **What it does not answer:** the effect of transaction summaries versus tabular/structured features on sparse FAR-Trans purchase nDCG@10. It uses Amazon reviews/metadata, four-item histories and NDCG@20. Four-item input construction and reductions in total training-set size are not pre-cutoff natural sparse-investor eligibility. Full LangPTune and NoProfile-CL have different fitted decoder weights and training settings; review information equivalence is not established. ([v2 §§4.1–4.5; Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2))

The decisive counterexample is **v2 Table 4: NoProfile-CL NDCG@20 0.0543 > 512-token LangPTune 0.0494**, Movie-TV/Llama. The absolute profile disadvantage is **0.0049**, roughly **9.0% relative to NoProfile-CL**, derived arithmetic without a reported significance test for that pair. Under the frozen decoder, NoProfile-0 **0.0372** exceeds zero-shot profile **0.0231**, while generator-trained RLSO gives **0.0377**; unresolved field/truncation parity prevents a fully information-matched interpretation. These findings motivate a two-sided question. They do not predict the sign on FAR-Trans. **All these numbers belong to the February 2025 manuscript titled *End-to-end Training for Recommendation with Language-based User Profiles*, not an independently read final CIKM article.** ([v2 Tables 2/4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2#S4.T4); [CIKM metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3746252.3761369))

### FLARKO is nearest in dataset and LLM application

FLARKO uses FAR-Trans transactions and market context, filtering inputs before a recommendation date and aligning Qwen3 with purchase/positive-return outcomes over 180 days. It therefore establishes same-domain feasibility much more directly than the review datasets. **What it answers:** how KG-grounded, KTO-trained LLM recommendations perform on purchase/profitability hit metrics. **What it does not answer:** whether narrating the same history improves a fixed ranker's sparse-user nDCG@10. Its personal and market KGs are structured **JSON-LD**; ten-week price summaries are numeric aggregates; its metrics are **Pref@3/Prof@3/Comb@3**. No sparse-history subgroup is evaluated. ([v1 §§3–4, 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15993v1#S3))

PKG/MKG/Combined/Nothing ablations change information rather than its encoding. Combined context is below PKG on preference and joint hits for every centralized model size, and **1.7B/Nothing** has the highest centralized **Comb@3 0.3718 ± 0.0547**. The table therefore does not justify assuming that richer context always improves personalized predictions. Exact cohort/candidate parity and metric denominators remain verification gaps. Original v1 was read; final ICAIF metadata was verified separately. ([v1 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15993v1#S7.T2); [publication metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3768292.3770399))

## Task1 motivates sparsity without fixing a future cohort

The local measurements are **full-release descriptive evidence**: 29,090 transaction customers; median **three total events/two Buys**; **21,237 (73.00%)** with 1–3 Buys, **23,663 (81.34%)** with 1–5 and **25,736 (88.47%)** with 1–10. These are overlapping event-count bands, not unique-asset counts. **360** customers in the 1–5 Buy band have more than ten total events. Low purchase count can therefore coexist with substantial sale history. ([Task1 sparse-user analysis](sparse_user_analysis.md))

The paper's “over 50% have three or fewer transactions” instead counts **all events across 2018–2022**. Neither that statistic nor Task1's whole-release Buy bands defines sparse membership at a historical prediction date. A globally active user can still be sparse at that date; a full-release buyer can have no prior purchases then. Zero full-release zero-Buy users does not rule out zero-history users at an earlier cutoff. Task1's **1–5 pre-cutoff Buy events** suggestion is provisional, not a validated optimum or official threshold. ([FAR-Trans §3.3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08692v1#S3.SS3.SSS2); [Task1 counting/eligibility discussion](sparse_user_analysis.md#why-these-are-not-the-official-sparse-user-population))

Local quality observations matter to information parity: repeated snapshots and three conflicting asset/date keys require declared handling; missing sector/industry fields cannot become invented prose; predicted customer fields and questionnaire/snapshot date inconsistencies do not establish historical availability. The release also reconstructs some early buys from end-period balances without a raw row-level reconstruction flag. These facts motivate provenance checks, not automatic deletion or a measured claim of leakage. Local **388,048 rows/320 traded ISINs** differ from paper **388,049/321**; the reasons remain unresolved. ([Task1 inventory](dataset_inventory.md); [Task1 quality analysis](data_quality_analysis.md); [FAR-Trans §3.3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08692v1#S3.SS3.SSS1))

## Pending controlled-comparison checklist

The checklist below identifies what a later comparison must specify to answer the representation question. **All boxes are pending requirements, not completed experimental actions.** It implements the control gaps identified in LangPTune, FLARKO and the financial benchmark; no model, prompt, threshold, date or official split is selected here.

- [ ] **Fixed users:** freeze the same eligible evaluation users/user–date instances for both arms. Define sparse membership using historical data only. Report exclusions and cohort sizes; distinguish sparse warm-start from zero-history users. If future activity is needed to score nDCG, state it as a separate evaluability condition, not the definition of sparsity.
- [ ] **Fixed candidates:** use the same candidate assets per user/date, availability rules, seen-item masking, and any sampling. Resolve invalid/duplicate recommendations consistently. Neither 320 locally traded nor 806 metadata assets automatically defines the historical candidate universe.
- [ ] **Fixed item information:** provide the same permitted asset attributes and time-valid snapshots to both representations and candidate encoders. Record missing/conflicting fields identically; do not grant only the summary access to external asset descriptions, future prices or complete-release metadata.
- [ ] **Fixed train/history window:** use identical pre-cutoff events and history duration, with the same timestamp boundary, event types and treatment of repetitions. Separate training, validation and future outcomes chronologically according to the forthcoming class protocol. Filter before fitting preprocessing, computing popularity or generating summaries.
- [ ] **Baseline information equivalence:** the structured comparator must be allowed the **same underlying event fields and metadata**, including amounts, units, timing and buy/sell status if the summary sees them. A summary using extra risk profiles, market context or external knowledge tests augmentation as well as representation. Conversely, compare against meaningful structured history rather than an artificially information-poor baseline. Compression losses are part of the treatment; unequal source access is a confound.
- [ ] **Fixed downstream ranker:** explicitly distinguish **identical frozen fitted weights** from **the same scoring architecture/objective with separate fitting**. Both can be informative, but make different claims. Declare representation-specific adapters and their capacity; hold candidate representations, tuning budget and fitting conditions comparable. Sharing the Mxbai name alone is not fixed-weight control.
- [ ] **Fixed purchase target:** clarify novel assets only versus repeat purchases, future horizon, Buy relevance, aggregation and zero-relevant-user treatment. Both arms use identical labels and **nDCG@10**. FAR-Trans's published new-to-user six-month task is not automatically the class task.
- [ ] **Pre-cutoff sparse counting unit:** declare all transaction events, Buy events or distinct bought assets, and whether a finite history window applies. If both buys and sells enter summaries, report total historical events as well as Buy counts. Recompute after the official cutoff/item scope is known; do not select on full-release activity.
- [ ] **Matched historical provenance:** treat reconstructed/adjusted events and estimated fields consistently in both arms. Separate an event's assigned date from evidence it was actually known then. Avoid verbalizing inferred holdings or risk traits as directly observed facts.
- [ ] **Controlled summary generation:** record model/version, prompt, decoding and length limits; ground summaries in permitted records and assess unsupported additions/omissions. Check external/pretraining knowledge asymmetry. Fluency or shorter reading time alone is not faithfulness or predictive accuracy.
- [ ] **Paired and temporally aware assessment:** compare nDCG differences on matched instances; account for recurrent users and overlapping windows when later estimating uncertainty. Clarify model/tuning randomness. No independent-replicate count or significance test is chosen from the paper's overlapping 61 windows by assumption.
- [ ] **Contextual baselines and interpretation:** retain popularity as a serious benchmark consideration given the published 0.2710 aggregate nDCG@10, while distinguishing such external baselines from the primary same-information representation contrast. State negative, null and positive results with their uncertainty and cohort scope.

These controls are **inferences for later preparation**, not procedures already validated for the exact RQ. LangPTune supplies partial frozen-decoder evidence but does not certify input parity; FLARKO changes context/supervision; FAR-Trans and Investors compare different model/data families. ([LangPTune v2 §§4.2, 4.5/Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2); [FLARKO §§3–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15993v1); [FAR-Trans §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08692v1#S5.SS2); [Investors §6](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3780097#sec-6))

## No official split or expected improvement is inferred

The class cutoff/split, horizon, sparse threshold, candidate policy, repeat-purchase rule and allowed feature sources remain pending. FAR-Trans's **61 temporal variants from 1 August 2019** and FLARKO's **December 2021–2022 test interval** are each source-specific protocols. Investors' additional last-origin statement does not resolve the exact date-list inconsistency. None is promoted to an official class split. Published aggregate LightGCN **0.3404** is not an expected sparse-cohort replication score. ([FAR-Trans §5.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08692v1#S5.SS2.SSS1); [FLARKO §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15993v1#S6.SS1); [Investors §6.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3780097#sec-6-1); [Task1 protocol status](dataset_inventory.md#sources-read-and-scope))

The RQ remains about **purchase prediction, not profit**. A positive nDCG@10 difference would support better ranking of subsequently bought assets under the declared protocol; it would not establish better investment returns, risk suitability, causal adoption, interpretability or welfare. Investors reports pooled nDCG/monthly-ROI correlation **−0.13** and cautions against treating one metric family as a proxy for the other, while explicitly retaining transaction evaluation as useful. ([Investors §§7, 12](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3780097#sec-7))

**Negative and null outcomes would be informative.** A negative result under matched conditions would show that the chosen summary pipeline loses or distorts useful predictive information in that cohort; it would not refute every possible language representation. A null estimate with wide uncertainty would be inconclusive, whereas a sufficiently precise near-zero estimate would constrain plausible benefit for the tested setting. A positive result would still require the control checks above before attributing it to representation. LangPTune's compression result makes this a substantive empirical question, not an exercise with a predetermined favorable answer. ([v2 §4.5.1/Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2#S4.T4))
