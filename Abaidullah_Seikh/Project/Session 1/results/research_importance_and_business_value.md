**RQ1:** Do LLM-generated transaction-history summaries improve nDCG@10 for predicting future asset purchases compared with structured-history features for FAR-Trans users with sparse histories?

## Research Importance

### Concise paragraph for Section 1

Sparse-history financial recommendation is a useful test of whether language-based representations preserve or improve limited behavioral evidence. FAR-Trans documents a long-tailed activity distribution, with over half its customers making at most three transactions across the observation period [1, §3.3, Fig. 2]. Its benchmark establishes purchase-ranking baselines, while FLARKO demonstrates LLM-based recommendation on FAR-Trans using structured knowledge graphs and purchase/profitability hit metrics [1, §5; 2, §§3–7]. Natural-language profiles can support inspectable recommendations [3], but their compression can reduce ranking accuracy: LangPTune's v2 direct-history comparator exceeds its summarized-profile pipeline in one Movie-TV comparison [4, Table 4]. Among these five studies, we did not find the proposed information-matched comparison for pre-cutoff sparse FAR-Trans users. Testing structured features against summaries, with a deterministic-text control and shared evaluation conditions, would clarify whether the chosen summary pipeline helps purchase ranking or discards useful evidence. Either outcome would bound the usefulness of language-based history representations in this financial setting, without implying a literature-wide novelty claim.

### Evidence and scope behind the argument

- **Meaningful population:** Task 1 measured a full-release median of **2 Buy events**, P75 **4**, and **23,663/29,090 customers (81.34%)** with 1–5 Buys. These local counts motivate a *provisional* low-activity band; they do not establish pre-cutoff eligibility. They differ in counting unit from the paper's total-transaction statistic. See [sparse_user_analysis.md](sparse_user_analysis.md).
- **Established knowledge:** [1] provides a common financial dataset and eleven non-LLM algorithms; [2] studies graph-grounded LLM recommendations; [3] studies review-based natural-language profiles and profile editing; [4] studies profile-generator/ranker optimization and the information bottleneck; [5] distinguishes transaction relevance from profitability. None of these contributions should be relabelled a controlled sparse-FAR-Trans summary result.
- **Nearest prior work:** LangPTune is nearest methodologically; FLARKO is nearest in dataset/application. LangPTune's accessible v2 Table 4 reports **NoProfile-CL NDCG@20 0.0543**, versus **0.0494** for the 512-token profile. These are Amazon Movie-TV results, with no significance claim for this exact pair; the direct input is item-metadata text, not the proposed financial structured vector. The final CIKM tables have not been independently checked.
- **Scientific contrast:** A-versus-C compares two practical representation pipelines using the same permissible source facts. B-versus-C narrows the comparison by retaining the same frozen text encoder and adapter architecture while removing LLM generation. It helps distinguish text encoding/formatting from generated abstraction, but does not prove an internal reasoning mechanism. Encoder differences and unequal retained information after compression remain part of the treatment.
- **Knowledge contribution:** estimate a paired ranking effect and its uncertainty for a declared population, source-information budget and historical protocol; document when compression, pretrained semantics or unsupported statements might explain the result. This is a scoped empirical contribution, not a promised improvement or general superiority of LLMs.

## Business Value

### Concise paragraph for Section 1

A financial recommendation product owner must decide whether adding an offline LLM-summary stage is worth its cost compared with maintaining structured customer-history features. A few purchases provide limited evidence for personalization, and FAR-Trans's strong popularity baseline cautions against assuming that a more complex representation adds value [1, §5]. Profile research suggests that language may organize behavior into a readable representation, but also documents information loss, hallucinations and latency [3, §§5–8; 4, §4.5]. Here, the proposed benefit is better ranking of future purchases from the same historical facts, not discovery of a customer's true goals or risk tolerance. A reliable improvement at acceptable measured cost would support further offline validation and a supervised product pilot; an adverse or sufficiently precise near-zero result would support retaining the simpler pipeline and avoiding generation and monitoring overhead. Because purchases are behavioral labels, improved nDCG@10 would not establish profitability, suitability, revenue or causal customer benefit [5, §12].

### Practical decision and costs to record

The stakeholder is the **product owner of an asset-discovery/recommendation service**, working with its engineering and model-review teams. This is a research decision about a ranking component, not personal financial advice or a live trading system. Possible benefits of readable histories are hypotheses transferred from review-domain studies, not demonstrated FAR-Trans outcomes. In the proposed experiment, summaries are internal model inputs; human-facing usefulness is not evaluated.

The additional work includes summary generation whenever history changes, text embedding, caching/version invalidation, factuality checks, monitoring and failure handling. Measure generation and embedding time, cache-hit and cold-path latency, storage, token volumes and billed compute alongside ranking time. Caching may move generation outside the request path; it does not eliminate refresh latency, stale summaries or generation cost. Operational risks include invented preferences, lost quantities/recency, identity exposure, and changed output after model revisions. The provisional **US$50 and 48 GPU-hour ceilings** are study planning limits, not an estimate of product ROI, an approved spend or measured expenditure. See [evaluation_plan.md](evaluation_plan.md).

## Positive-result implications

**Research — brief Section 5 paragraph.** If C exceeds A with a positive paired uncertainty interval on the preregistered sparse cohort, the finding would support this specific summary-plus-encoder pipeline under the tested information and scoring conditions. A C-over-B advantage would provide additional evidence for generated abstraction beyond deterministic text encoding; a C-over-A gain without a C-over-B gain would leave that attribution unsupported. Neither pattern establishes universal LLM superiority, an effect in every market regime, or an improvement caused solely by summarization independent of encoder choices [2–4].

**Business — brief Section 5 paragraph.** A reproducible gain would justify further validation only if measured generation/refresh cost, latency, failure rates and factuality are acceptable to the product owner. It would support considering a controlled, supervised pilot rather than automatic deployment. Financial suitability and causal product outcomes would require separate evidence; nDCG@10 alone cannot justify claims of greater returns or revenue [5].

## Negative-result implications

**Research — brief Section 5 paragraph.** A negative C-minus-A effect would show that the tested summary pipeline is less effective than the structured comparator in this setting; loss of detail is one explanation to investigate, not an established cause. If B also outperforms C, that would strengthen the interpretation that the generation stage adds little or removes useful information. A confidence interval spanning meaningful gains and losses is inconclusive, not evidence of equivalence. A sufficiently narrow near-zero interval would constrain plausible benefit for the tested setup, consistent with treating compression as a genuine trade-off [3, §7; 4, §4.5].

**Business — brief Section 5 paragraph.** An adverse or sufficiently precise negligible result would support retaining structured features and avoiding an unsubstantiated generation, maintenance and monitoring expense. An uncertain result would support postponing expansion or collecting better evaluation evidence, rather than declaring the two approaches equivalent. This decision concerns predictive usefulness and operational burden; it establishes neither a financial loss avoided nor a causal business outcome.
